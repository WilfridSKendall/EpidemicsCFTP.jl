====== 2 Algorithmic Time ======
Created Thursday 24 April 2014

===== Evolution in algorithmic time =====
The Poisson(α) and Poisson(β) incident patterns evolve //in algorithmic time// ("orthogonal" to process time!). In one possible variant, they evolve in continuous time as independent "spatial" (dimension //d//=1) immigration-death processes with unit death rates, and with immigration rates per unit time being α and β respectively. in another, they evolve in discrete time, alternately replacing all potential removal incidents and all potential infection incidents. In this second variant it is useful to track down the continuous process time axis, treating potential incidents "pixel by pixel". 

==== Reversibility ====
In the first case (continuous algorithmic time), reversibility follows rather directly from reversibility of spatial immigration-death processes, and CLAIM 1 in Section 1, that a realization of the SIR epidemic can be achieved as a measurable function of the Poisson equilibrium. Side-note: here we will end up dealing with CFTP for a process (//X//, //F//(//X//)), where //F// is a measurable function of the Markov chain //X//. This is somewhat remniscent of an ongoing programme of immersion coupling for nilpotent diffusions (recent overviews are given in Kendall, 2010, 2014).
In the second case, one has to use notions of dynamic reversibility.

==== Conditioning on removal times ====
Our objective is to obtain a Markov chain whose equilibrum distribution is the conditional distribution of the SIR epidemic conditional on all individuals being infected, and all removals being observed to occur at exactly specified times. (Variations on this may be feasible, at the price of additional complexity.)
We use a fundamental fact about reversible Markov chains: restriction of state-space leads to equilibria which are conditional distributions. One simply needs to check that the Markov chain in the restricted state-space is irreducible and positive-recurrent.

=== CLAIM 2 ===
This applies if we restrict the state-space by fixing all removal times.

=== CLAIM 3 ===
The relevant restrictions on the Markov chain in algorithmic time are: proposals to add potential removals are refused if they immediately result in actual removals (which is to say, if the potential removal is placed on the infected part of an individual timeline). Proposals to delete potential removals are refused only if the removal in question is actual, hence one of the observed removals. Proposals to remove potential infections are refused if they immediately result in the epidemic not infecting the entire population. Proposals to add potential infections are refused if they would become actual infections //and// they themselves would lead to infection of a timeline //before// a potential, not actual, removal; they are also refused if they would become actual infections //and// they would lead indirectly to  infection of a timeline //before// a potential, not actual, removal.
If a proposal to remove a potential infection is refused, we say that the retained potential infection is //perpetuated//. Note that perpetuated potential infections must be actual infections.

==== Simulation ====
Again, to make sure we have a secure understanding of this, we should consider how to build a robust simulation programme for the algorithmic time evolution. In particular, we need to check reversibility and conditioning arguments, as well as evaluating the possibility of monotonicity structures.
